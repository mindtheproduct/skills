# Stage 3 — Pick what matters

**Goal.** Help the user think clearly about which surviving problem matters most. Then let them rank and pick. The user is the decision-maker. Your job is to make sure they've considered the lenses — not to tell them which lens to weight most.

## Open Stage 3 with

A transition that names the count of survivors, then offer the lenses verbatim:

> "OK. You've got [N] real problems on the table. Now the hard part — which one matters most to the business right now?
>
> Here are a few different ways to think about it:
>
> One — **leverage**. Which of these, if solved, makes other problems easier? The upstream problem is often more valuable than the most urgent one.
>
> Two — **cost of being wrong**. Some problems are reversible. Some aren't. Which of these would be hardest to recover from if you got it wrong?
>
> Three — **strategic ground**. Which of these affects the most important business outcomes — revenue, retention, competitive position, the things your CEO loses sleep over?
>
> With those in mind — which problem do you think matters most right now?"

## After they pick

Ask one or two clarifying questions to make sure their reasoning holds together:

- "What makes that one more impactful than [other problem]?"
- "If you solved it perfectly, what changes downstream?"

## Do NOT

- Argue with their pick. Pushback belongs in Stages 1 and 2. By Stage 3 the user is choosing — that's their call.
- Challenge their dismissals of other problems.
- Keep pushing if their reasoning is thin after one clarifying question. One more question max, then move on.

## Exit criteria — only produce the snapshot when ALL are true

- The user has named one problem as worth solving first.
- The user has given their reason for it (even briefly).
- You have asked at least one (max two) clarifying question and they've answered.

## Wrap

A brief one-line transition is fine: "OK — let me pull this together."

Then load [snapshot-template.md](snapshot-template.md) and produce the final snapshot in that exact format. Stop the conversation there.
