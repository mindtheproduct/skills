# Snapshot template

Output the snapshot in this **exact markdown format**, filling in the placeholders with the user's actual content from the conversation.

````markdown
# Make the Call — Your snapshot

## The decision
<one sentence restating what they're wrestling with, in their words>

## The asks you were hearing — and the problems underneath
- **Ask:** <ask 1, in their words>
  **Problem underneath:** <the actual problem they articulated underneath, in their words>
- **Ask:** <ask 2>
  **Problem underneath:** <…>
<one entry per ask the user mentioned in Stage 1>

## Stress-test results
- **[SURVIVED] <short problem name>** — <one short line on why it passed>
- **[KILLED] <short problem name>** — <one short line on why it was killed>
<one entry per problem from Stage 1>

## The problem worth solving first
**<the problem the user picked in Stage 3>**

<1–2 sentences in their words on why they think it matters most>

## One question to sit with before you commit
> <a single sharp question, tailored to this specific decision — not generic. It should reflect a specific tension or blind spot that came up in the conversation. End with a question mark.>

---

The call is yours to make. But you'll make it sharper now than you would have an hour ago.
````

## Rules for the snapshot

- Use the user's words wherever possible. They did the thinking — you're capturing it cleanly.
- Do **not** invent asks, problems, or stress-test results that weren't actually discussed.
- The reflective question at the end is the most important part. It should be specific to the conversation, not a generic "what does success look like?". It should probe a real tension that surfaced — something the user might dodge if they don't sit with it.
- Tone for any text fields: direct, conversational, no fluff. No "great", "fantastic", "you've done a wonderful job".

## After the snapshot

Don't continue the conversation. The practice is done. If the user replies, you can briefly acknowledge — but don't restart the stages or start advising on implementation. The next decision deserves its own session.
